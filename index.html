What it looks like right now

You are making your biggest decisions on a number that only tells you about today

You have been dreaming forever about this business making money, and now it does, and you cannot see what it looks like ninety days from now.

  • You decide on the hire from how the balance looked that morning.
  • The balance tells you almost nothing about the end of the month, so you check it again.
  • You tell yourself you will go through it this week, and you have been telling yourself that for a month.

You make the call from the balance, a gut read, and a spreadsheet you built in January and stopped updating in March. Your bookkeeper sends a profit and loss report that tells you what already happened, and you still say "I don't know my numbers." Every week you don't look makes the next look harder, because the longer you wait, the worse you fear it is.

I am not avoiding my finances, I am avoiding my shame around my finances. That is my own line, and what it costs is every decision I did not get to make on purpose.

What you see when you open it

One calm view of the year ahead, current from the QuickBooks you already use

Stellium is where you see what is coming before you decide. It connects to the QuickBooks you already use, so there is nothing to migrate and your bookkeeper keeps doing the work they do now.

It takes what your books already know and carries it forward 30, 60, 90 days, and out to a year, with what is moving your cash and when. So when the hire comes up, you are looking at what happens if you make it, and you are the one deciding.

How it works

Three moves, and you are deciding from what is coming

  1. Connect QuickBooks

    You do it once. Stellium reads your books, so the view starts from what is in your accounts today and updates as your books do.

  2. See what is coming

    Cash on hand, what is coming in, what is going out, and the low point, laid out so you know whether you can pay yourself before you get there.

  3. Run the decision first

    The hire, the marketing spend, the client who may not re-enroll. Watch each one move your cash before it happens, and then make the call on purpose.

Your numbers stay yours

You approve the connection on Intuit’s own screen, and you never give Stellium your bank login or your QuickBooks password.

You can disconnect it from inside QuickBooks any time you want, and the connection ends there.

Who it's for

Built for the founder who is past survival and before scale

Stellium is for you if

  • You run a service business (coaching, consulting, an agency, a group practice) bringing in roughly $100K to $1M.
  • You use QuickBooks, you may have a bookkeeper, and you still feel like you are flying blind.
  • You want to pay yourself a real salary, make the next hire, or take a break, and you want to see it work on paper first.

Stellium is the wrong fit if

  • You are running a hobby business, a side hustle, or a pre-revenue startup.
  • You need a fractional CFO or full strategic financial leadership, which Stellium does not replace.
  • Your books live somewhere other than QuickBooks today.

About

Meet Christine

Christine Walsh spent 12 years on Wall Street, rising to SVP in global portfolio management, where she moved nine-figure institutional portfolios. Then in 2010, unemployed in a Colorado ski town and facing foreclosure, she sat there as the supposed money expert, afraid to look at her own bank account.

She built the model she had never been given. Across 20 years in entrepreneurial finance, she has sat inside women founders' businesses building their cash flow forecasts with them, week after week, and in 2016 she founded RebelRise Finance. The method inside Stellium is the one she runs weekly with her private clients.

She wrote the #1 bestseller Be the Queen of Your Money, hosts the Made for Money podcast, and is a Master Certified Money Coach.

When she's out of the numbers, she and her husband Steve are somewhere on the road in their RV, four to five months a year, with four plants counting on them to come home.

Why I built it

I go first. I was the money expert who couldn't open her own bank account, and the tool that got me out is the one inside Stellium.

I built this to hold the numbers the way an accountant would, the forward view the way a financial advisor would, and what comes up in your body when you look, the way a coach would. The financial industry was never designed with women founders in mind, and I spent twenty years inside their businesses knowing there had to be a better way.

What I love about Stellium? Whatever the numbers turn out to be, seeing them hands you the chance to change them, and that is where your choice and your power return back to you.
